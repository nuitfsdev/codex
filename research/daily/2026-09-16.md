# Daily Proposal Research — 2026-09-16

## Material update: CoVeR as a new efficiency baseline and design signal

**Paper:** CoVeR: Coverage-Based Token Pruning for Multi-View 3D Reasoning in VLMs (arXiv:2609.08345, submitted 2026-09-08). Public code: `humansensinglab/CoVeR`.

### Why it matters
CoVeR is training-free and deterministic. It selects multi-view visual tokens using 3D spatial coverage rather than learned semantic importance. It evaluates on ScanQA, SQA3D and OpenEQA and reports that ~8% token retention preserves 93.5% of full-token performance. At a 9% token budget the reported efficiency is 13.3x fewer LLM TFLOPs, 10.7x smaller KV cache and 2.9x inference speedup with ~1.1% relative accuracy loss. This is directly relevant to the proposal because it provides strong evidence that preserving *coverage/diversity* can be more important than conventional learned importance when pruning multi-view 3D evidence.

### Novelty boundary
CoVeR operates **after rendering at visual-token level** and deliberately uses geometry-only coverage; it does not perform question-conditioned object/subscene selection and does not distinguish explicit seed objects from unnamed QA-support objects. Therefore it does not replace QASER/SAFER-QA, but it is now a strong efficiency baseline and a strong prior for the support-budget allocator.

## Candidate: QASER-Cov — Question-Conditioned Support Selection with Coverage Reserve

**Research Hypothesis:** A strict object budget should not be allocated entirely by query relevance. Hard-retaining seed objects and high-confidence relation-compatible supports while reserving part of the object budget for spatially diverse coverage can reduce the risk that semantic/query ranking removes an unnamed answer/support object. This transfers CoVeR's coverage principle from post-render tokens to pre-render GT objects while remaining QA-conditioned.

**Stage changed:** question-conditioned object/subscene selection, before PoseAlign-T / Abstract 3D rendering.

**Object-selection rule:**
1. Parse/ground explicit seed objects from the question; hard-retain them.
2. Generate global relation/question-conditioned candidates without universal KNN/radius gating.
3. Allocate K object slots in two pools: relevance/relation pool and coverage-reserve pool.
4. Fill the coverage reserve with farthest/diverse candidates in 3D box-center/extent space, conditioned on not duplicating already retained spatial regions.
5. After PoseAlign-T, refine viewpoint-dependent relations and drop incompatible candidates; optionally refill freed slots.

**Supporting-context rule:** supporting objects need not occur lexically in the question. They may enter through relation endpoints or through the coverage reserve. This is specifically intended to protect against cases where the true answer/support has low text similarity and is not locally nearest to a seed.

**Expected benefit:** lower context-loss risk than pure SemanticTopK / CAPruner-endpoint ranking / nearest-neighbor expansion at the same object budget; likely strongest at aggressive K.

**Implementation difficulty:** low-medium. Requires only GT boxes, seed/relation parser already planned, and deterministic spatial diversity selection. No training required.

**Compute / VRAM:** selector is CPU/lightweight and effectively negligible GPU VRAM. Main VRAM remains the <=7B 2D VLM. Coverage reserve should reduce rendering/VLM load relative to Full Scene.

**Data / metrics:** ScanQA + SQA3D; downstream EM/CIDEr/LLM-Match as appropriate; Object Reduction Ratio; Question Entity Coverage; Context Benefit Rate; Support Ablation Sensitivity; QA-vs-object-budget; spatial coverage of selected objects. Supporting-Context Retention remains proxy-only without explicit support GT.

**Critical ablation:** Full Scene vs SemanticTopK vs Seed+KNN vs relation-only TopK vs relation+coverage reserve, matched K={5,10,20}; then +PoseAlign-T. Sweep coverage reserve fraction (e.g. 0, 20%, 40%). Post-render CoVeR can be an orthogonal efficiency ablation, not part of novelty.

**Risk:** geometry coverage can deliberately retain irrelevant clutter. It should be a reserve/tie-breaker rather than the main selector. If relation+coverage does not improve the QA/object Pareto frontier over relation-only selection, do not retain it as a contribution.

**Scores (1–5):** Novelty 3.3 standalone / 3.7 inside QASER; Expected Accuracy Gain 3.8; Feasibility 4.8; Compute Cost 4.9 (higher=cheaper); Fit 5.0; Evidence Strength 4.8.

**Object-selection targets (to test, not claims):** Object Reduction Ratio 50–85%; Question Entity Coverage >=95%; Supporting-Context Retention unknown/proxy-only; context-loss risk medium-low relative to pure relevance ranking.

**Publication potential:** moderate-good if matched-budget experiments show that a small coverage reserve materially improves QA preservation over pure relevance/relation ranking, especially for unnamed answer/support cases.

## Top candidate this week
**SAFER-QA / QASER, now with a coverage-reserve allocator as the most promising new refinement.**

Current preferred implementation:
`seed retrieval -> question/relation-conditioned global candidate generation -> relevance/relation allocation + small spatial-coverage reserve -> PoseAlign-T directional refinement -> selective Abstract3D render -> <=7B VLM`.

Reason: this directly addresses the recent concern that the answer/support is not guaranteed to be near the seed or highly semantically similar to the question. CoVeR supplies strong empirical evidence on ScanQA/SQA3D that spatial coverage is valuable under aggressive evidence budgets, while the thesis still differentiates itself by moving selection to the **object level before rendering** and making it **question/support aware**.
