## Focused candidate: WAC — Witness Annotation & Completeness Protocol (EVALUATION, not novel method)
Hypothesis: distinguishing explicit seed, unnamed answer, supporting relation anchors and distractors reveals whether semantic Top-K fails due to context loss, and permits an objective go/no-go decision for SAFER-QA.
Changed stage: dataset annotation / selector evaluation only. Object selection: fixed annotated scene table, frozen BERT/MiniLM/BGE question-to-label Top-K; role-aware candidate selector as competing method, not GT-driven. Supporting rule: independently annotate minimum sufficient seed–answer–support witness sets, with multiple valid sets allowed; count/negative queries flagged as requiring global completeness certificates. Strict |S|<=K before rendering; no full-scene rendering in main method.
Expected benefit: diagnostic confidence and unbiased baseline; **no guaranteed accuracy gain**. Difficulty medium (annotation quality); no training, CPU-only annotation and geometric checks, near-zero extra GPU VRAM; 2D VLM <=7B only at separate scheduled inference run.
Data: 50–100 ScanQA validation questions stratified by unnamed answer, same-class ambiguity, counting, negative, multi-hop; SQA3D when annotation feasible. Metrics ORR=1-|S|/N, QEC, unnamed Answer-Witness Recall, independent-GT Supporting-Context Retention (otherwise proxy), complete-witness coverage, context loss, EM/CIDEr, scene-clustered CI, latency, peak VRAM. Compare same K=10, M=1 renderer/prompt/model/calls, later K={5,10,20}, M={1,2}; remove-support vs remove-distractor, label-only vs mesh-only, oracle only as upper bound. Risk: nonunique minimal supports, annotation leakage, counterfactual occlusion, missing complete-scene evidence; publication low standalone, high utility for rigorous thesis experiments.
Score /5 (higher compute=cheaper): Novelty 1.0, Expected Accuracy Gain 1.0, Feasibility 4.7, Compute 4.9, Fit 5.0, Evidence 4.2. Context-loss HIGH K=5 for counting/negation/multi-hop; ORR 50–85% and QEC>=95% are unvalidated targets.
